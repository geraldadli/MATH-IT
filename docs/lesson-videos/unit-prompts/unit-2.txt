UNIT TOPICS
Two-step problems; Missing numbers; Estimation; Word problems; Spotting mistakes

LEARNING GOAL
Introduce choosing operations from stories, following brackets, finding missing numbers, estimating, and checking mistakes.

AUDITION LINE
“Good detectives explain their thinking. Let us investigate five useful strategies.”

SETTING
A detective notebook with a story card, a two-step lock, a missing-number box, a number line, and a calculation to check.

MATHEMATICAL ACCURACY
Do not choose operations from isolated keywords. Brackets determine the first step in the shown examples. Use ≈ for an estimate and = for exact equality. Label incorrect work as a claim until it is corrected. Regrouping a ten changes the tens count.

TIMED STORYBOARD

SCENE 1 · 0:00–0:06 · Think like a detective
Visual and animation: Draw a notebook and a magnifying glass. Write the unit title in a clear hand-drawn heading.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
Unit 2
Algebra & reasoning
Narration: “Good detectives explain their thinking. Let us investigate five useful strategies.”

SCENE 2 · 0:06–0:26 · Understand a word problem
Visual and animation: Show small pictures of joining objects, taking some away, and repeated equal groups. Then align bars of 17 and 11 stickers to reveal their gap.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
Join: +
Take away or compare: −
Equal groups: ×
17 − 11 = 6
Narration: “Picture the story. Do things join, leave, repeat, or need comparing? Seventeen stickers compared with eleven means six more. We subtract to find the difference. The word more does not always mean add.”

SCENE 3 · 0:26–0:44 · Solve two steps in order
Visual and animation: Write the expression, circle only the brackets, replace them with 9, then complete the multiplication. Keep each line aligned beneath the previous one.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
(5 + 4) × 3
9 × 3 = 27
Narration: “For a two-step clue, work inside the brackets first. Five plus four makes nine. Replace the brackets with nine, then multiply by three. The final answer is twenty-seven.”

SCENE 4 · 0:44–1:02 · Find a missing number
Visual and animation: Draw a part-whole bar for an unknown amount plus 9 equaling 23. Undo the addition. Then show six equal groups totaling 30 to find the second missing number.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
□ + 9 = 23 → □ = 14
6 × □ = 30 → □ = 5
Narration: “A missing number must make the equation true. Undo adding nine with subtraction: the missing number is fourteen. For six times something equals thirty, divide. The missing number is five.”

SCENE 5 · 1:02–1:22 · Estimate with nearby tens
Visual and animation: Place 37 between 30 and 40, and 24 between 20 and 30, with equal tick spacing. Move each to the nearer ten. Use an approximate-equality symbol for the original sum.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
37 → 40
24 → 20
37 + 24 ≈ 60
Halfway? Round up.
Narration: “Estimate with nearby tens. Thirty-seven rounds to forty; twenty-four rounds to twenty. Forty plus twenty makes sixty, so the total is about sixty. For whole numbers halfway between tens, round up.”

SCENE 6 · 1:22–1:46 · Spot a mistake and think
Visual and animation: Show a clearly labeled claim with the answer 35. Ask what was missed and pause for 4 seconds. Exchange the 15 ones to correct the total to 45. Finish by contrasting five groups of four with five plus four.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
Claim: 27 + 18 gives 35
Check: 27 + 18 = 45
5 × 4 = 20; 5 + 4 = 9
Narration: “Check this claim: twenty-seven plus eighteen equals thirty-five. What was missed? The extra ten! The answer is forty-five. Also check the sign: five times four means twenty, not nine.”
PAUSE: Immediately after “What was missed?”, hold silently for 4 seconds. Then continue the remaining narration and reveal the answer. This pause is included in the scene time.

SCENE 7 · 1:46–2:00 · Ready for the unit quizzes
Visual and animation: Draw five small reminder icons: story, brackets, box, rounded number, and check mark. End with the quiz invitation.
On-screen text (reveal progressively):
Understand → Work in steps → Check
Try the Unit 2 quizzes
Narration: “Understand the story, follow each step, and check your result. Put missing numbers back into their equations to test them. Try the Unit Two quizzes!”
